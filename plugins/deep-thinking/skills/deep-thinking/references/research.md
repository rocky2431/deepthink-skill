# Deep Research within Deep Thinking

Use this when external evidence can clarify the question, test a consequential
claim, broaden the available perspectives, or distinguish possible solutions.
This part works iteratively with Clear My Mind: evidence can revise the question itself.

## Input and place in the workflow

Start from the live question, purpose, goals and constraints, current explanations,
consequential unknowns, and intended output in the existing `THOUGHTS.md` or conversation.
Follow the topic and file paths established by [the main workflow](../SKILL.md).
Research develops a factor and evidence map, returns useful batches to `THOUGHTS.md`,
and supplies the supported material for synthesis into `RESULT.md` at delivery.
Do not create a separate research database or a lasting report for every agent or batch.
Respect an agreed conversation-only or alternative output arrangement.

Use available context rather than repeating clarification. A small exploratory
search can establish unfamiliar terminology. Return to clarification when the
missing input is a personal meaning, objective, or value choice that changes the
investigation; the assistant cannot settle that choice by collecting more sources.

## Choose the next investigation

Translate the current uncertainty into a researchable question. Explain its relation
to the overall inquiry and what finding could alter the view, reveal a missing factor,
or open a useful possibility. Compare its value with other consequential gaps before
deepening the branch; a small Alpha probe need not prove its eventual value. Keep
factual questions, interpretations, value choices, and practical feasibility distinguishable.
Literature can inform a value tradeoff without choosing the user's values for them.

Relate the user's current understanding and intended Beta to the relevant external
baseline: established knowledge and practice, assumptions, disagreements, and limits.
Use research to close consequential gaps when Beta needs support. Where the relevant
Beta is understood, investigate candidate Alpha through overlooked evidence, adjacent
fields, alternative explanations, or new applications. State what each candidate adds
to personal understanding separately from its external distinctiveness. A mature
method can be personal Alpha while remaining external Beta; personal assimilation
does not remove an idea's external Alpha status.

For an external Alpha/Beta claim, identify the relevant field, participants, context,
and time. Examine evidence of how widespread or distinctive the idea or practice is;
failing to find it in a search does not establish rarity. Assess truth, usefulness,
and feasibility separately: a minority idea can be wrong, and a mainstream one can
be sound or flawed. Seek the distinctive perspectives implied by "always thinking
like 1%" without claiming a measured percentile or requiring certainty to explore.

Use the direction already agreed in the conversation. Briefly explain a substantial
new research direction before pursuing it; do not ask for approval of every query.
When the framing is unfamiliar, a small exploratory search may uncover terminology,
prior work, or alternatives needed to formulate a better question. Bring a material
reframing back to the user before building a solution around it.

## Map the factors that matter

Develop a compact, revisable factor map with the user. Examine both the focal person
or initiative (goals, capabilities, resources, constraints) and the present external
environment (relevant conditions, participants, incentives, timing, and changes).
For a product question, include users, competitors, substitutes, and complementary
actors. Derive other inquiries' dimensions from the question rather than imposing a
business checklist. Investigate the situation's significance as well as its mechanism.

Connect the two sides to establish the subject's current position or niche: whom it
serves, what it depends on, how it compares or cooperates with others, and which
opportunities or constraints follow. Treat proposed advantages as hypotheses until
supported. Explain which changes in the subject or environment would change the
judgment; an internally coherent proposal still needs to fit its actual setting.

For consequential factors, show why they matter, what is established and from which
sources, their relationships to other factors, and the remaining gaps. A small table
in the existing thought draft is enough. Prioritize factors whose plausible variation
could change the conclusion; many settled minor factors do not resolve one pivotal gap.

The owner's "95% confidence" goal means near-complete command of relevant factors
and information under current conditions, not a 95% probability of success. Support
that goal by exposing coverage, understanding, and uncertainty. Collected material,
verified facts, and supported causal explanations are different kinds of progress.

Do not invent an exact coverage percentage when the full factor set is unknown.
If a bounded coverage measure is useful, state its scope, denominator, evidence,
and limits; completion of an agreed list does not establish coverage of all reality.
Challenge the map itself through outside perspectives, contrary cases, and alternative
framings. Newly discovered factors can legitimately expand the research still needed.

Associate consequential factors with the questions that led to their investigation,
the supporting sources, and their effect on the current account. Reorganize the
outline when evidence reveals a more useful relationship or problem definition.
When discussion repeats known points or an Alpha probe could broaden the inquiry,
inspect relevant material already retrieved but not yet used: it may expose a missing
factor, connection, or question. Explain the proposed addition and let user steering
change the focus. A table or outline in `THOUGHTS.md` can serve this shared structure.

Source: [Co-STORM, sections 3.1–3.5 and 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15232) describes
a shared mind map connected to retrieved information, questions from unused material,
and a cited report built from that structure. Its 20-person human evaluation concerns
information-seeking experience, not durable gains in personal reasoning. Borrow the
information organization and discovery moves; do not copy its automatic turn-taking
to answer for the user, advance a user-dependent choice, or continue after a pause.

## Divide independent investigations when useful

Use multiple contributors when factual branches, competing explanations, or evidence
channels can be investigated independently. Give each a bounded question, relevant
goals and constraints, and necessary original material. Use the
[independent-contributor guidance](roundtable.md#form-and-compare-positions) for initial
context and disclose the actual arrangement. Ask each to return direct sources or
observations, support and limits, unresolved conflicts, and implications for the question.

The coordinator checks decisive material and integrates the findings into the factor
map. Different investigators do not make repeated copies of a study independent
evidence. Parallel research need not become a debate; use a roundtable only when
comparing a consequential disagreement or alternative would help. If delegation is
unavailable, identify the work as a single-agent investigation.

## Gather evidence that bears on the judgment

- Prefer primary sources for concrete claims: original studies, official documents,
  data, and observable implementations. Use credible syntheses to orient a field
  and understand disagreement. Trace consequential claims to their actual support.
- Search for the strongest competing explanation, disconfirming evidence, failures,
  and boundary conditions as well as support. Follow relevant adjacent disciplines
  or stakeholders when they could expose an overlooked assumption.
- Check freshness when it matters. Distinguish publication dates, event dates,
  versions, and the date you verified the source. Record material coverage limits.
- Separate a product's advertised feature from a demonstrated effect. For studies,
  inspect the method, participants, comparison, measured outcome, and limitations
  before generalizing. A preference measure does not establish reasoning improvement.
- Distinguish independent evidence from pages repeating the same source. A direct
  primary source may settle a narrow fact; disputed judgments need the relevant
  competing evidence, not an arbitrary source count.
- Open important sources and verify that cited passages support the associated
  claim. Treat snippets and generated summaries as leads. If access is unavailable,
  identify the limitation rather than treating the content as verified.

For a consequential claim, connect the directly inspected evidence to the inference
and its effect on the current judgment. Separate what was observed in the source
from the assumptions needed to transfer it to this situation. Authority establishes
provenance, not applicability; inspect the relevant methods and contrary evidence.
State what evidence or changed condition would make you revise the assessment.

When the user wants to verify consequential evidence themselves, provide a usable
route to the original material, relevant passage or observation, and the assumptions
to check. Keep evidence worth investigating, assistant verification, user verification,
and an actual judgment update distinct. Record the user's verification and resulting
assessment only when they report or demonstrate them; do not require them to audit
every routine fact before independent work can continue.

Compare how well verified evidence supports the current account and meaningful
alternatives. Explain why confidence should strengthen, weaken, or remain unchanged;
an update can be partial. The user decides their own assessment and whether it warrants
a direction change given their goals and constraints. Do not invent numerical updates.

Keep compact notes in the existing thought draft. A table of claim, direct source/date,
support and limits, and effect on the choice is useful for several competing claims.
Do not create an evidence database by default. Agent analyses are interpretations;
several agents citing the same study still provide one evidential source.
Express uncertainty with its reasons; use numerical confidence only with a defensible
basis. Leave conflicting evidence visible and weight it by relevance and quality.

When competing accounts remain, identify an observation they predict differently
and seek the smallest feasible check. If the dispute concerns values, make the
tradeoff explicit for the user. The aim is a judgment they can inspect and challenge;
persuasive language or their agreement does not establish correctness.

When investigating success or failure patterns, define the comparison group, time
window, and outcome before making rate claims. Distinguish observed case counts from
population rates when the denominator is incomplete. Examine contrasting outcomes,
including survivors, and differences between their conditions and the current case.
Trace proposed triggers, intermediate effects, and feedback loops; identify the
evidence and alternative explanations for consequential causal links. A plausible
postmortem story is a hypothesis to examine, not proof of why the outcome occurred.

## Revise a candidate against the evidence

When research produces a candidate question, explanation, or solution, compare it
with the literature or observations before treating its rationale as established.
Challenge the consequential gap: whether an allegedly new idea is already known,
whether the proposed mechanism fits the evidence, or whether feasible observations
could distinguish it from an alternative. Develop speculative Alpha without requiring
it to be ready for commitment; scrutinize stronger claims accordingly.

Explain which finding warrants narrowing, revising, retaining, or abandoning a claim.
Keep a proposed revision attributable to the assistant until the user actually adopts
it. A changed problem definition may return the workflow to clarification; an evidence
gap may require another investigation. Recheck the claims affected by a substantive
revision before presenting the candidate as ready.

Source: [The Socratic Challenger](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1913451/full),
Methods, "GenAI-enabled workflow intervention," describes eight stages including
literature checks, a knowledge-gap challenge, revision, and expert feedback.
Discussion, "Interpreting self-report outcomes and strengthening measurement,"
limits the interpretation of its 45-student ratings and written reflections.
Borrow the evidence-to-revision cycle, not a mandatory eight-stage sequence, named
tools, or a claim of demonstrated general reasoning improvement. Its AI-off literature
work and human feedback cannot be reported as performed by the user merely because
an assistant checked a source. When actual expert feedback is needed, another model's
opinion does not supply it. Follow the verification distinctions above.

## Return useful batches to the user

After a coherent batch, explain what was learned, which assumption it supports or
challenges, and which factor gaps it closes or reveals in the whole inquiry. Show
the next transition: investigate a pivotal gap, compare alternatives, or converge.
Cite sources near claims and mark proposed revisions as yours.

Update `THOUGHTS.md` after a material batch: preserve the research question or claim,
direct source and date, actual observation, applicability assumptions, support and
counterevidence, effect on the judgment, and the remaining gap or next transition.
Reuse the existing factor and evidence entries rather than transcribing all results.
Record assistant verification, user-reported verification, and the user's actual
judgment update separately; an assistant's completed check is not the user's assent.

Ask one focused question when the next step needs the user's interpretation, values,
or choice, and wait before making that dependent judgment. Otherwise return the
synthesis or proceed to the needed evidence; do not prolong a completed branch with
a compulsory question. Keep the user involved at meaningful changes rather than
every search result. Record actual responses without inventing agreement.

## Form a warranted outcome

Stop a branch when it is sufficiently resolved for the current purpose, new sources
add no meaningful information, or the remaining uncertainty needs a different method.
Repeated copies of one source do not establish coverage. State whether the claim is
supported, conditional, or still unresolved; stopping a search is not resolving a claim.
Name the missing evidence and a useful next test. Return to clarification when research
exposes a different question; do not preserve an outline at the expense of the evidence.

Before presenting a solution as ready, make any unresolved factor that could overturn
it explicit. When observation, an interview, or a small experiment is needed to learn
more, present it as research still to do rather than declaring the coverage goal met.

For a practical solution, connect the recommendation to the user's goals and
constraints. Include the meaningful alternatives, decisive tradeoffs, prerequisites,
material costs or risks, and a small test with observable success and failure signals.
Label recommendations as provisional where their premises remain unverified. A plan
is a proposal until tested; implementation requires the user's applicable authority.

For an argument or article, preserve the strongest objection, evidence boundaries,
and unresolved interpretations through drafting. Keep the user involved in the
position and expression, including choosing to retain an open question.

At synthesis, turn the updated account into a candidate outcome for the agreed
purpose. A mature candidate can enter [adversarial review](roundtable.md#review-a-mature-candidate)
without first holding a roundtable. At delivery, synthesize the supported conclusion,
decisive evidence, alternatives, qualifications, and material unresolved issues into
`RESULT.md` under the main file contract. A conditional or interim result must retain
that status. The result should stand on its own; the working history stays in
`THOUGHTS.md`. A research batch alone does not close the whole inquiry.
