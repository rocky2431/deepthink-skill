# Methods for Clarification and Convergence

Select a method for the present obstacle. Explain the useful move in everyday
language and put it into the dialogue; do not teach a methods course or run every
method in sequence. Use answers already available before asking another question.
The sources below establish a method's provenance and scope. Adapting them to this
skill is a design judgment, not evidence that the skill improves thinking.

## Guided discovery: turn answers into understanding

Use when meanings, assumptions, or the relationship between observations and a
strongly held view need examination. Ask about something the user can answer,
listen for unexpected information, summarize what matters, and connect it back to
the original concern. Draw on concrete examples before requesting abstractions.
Offer a synthesis for correction; invite the user's own synthesis when it would
advance their understanding. Do not steer toward a predetermined change of mind.

For example: "You described two cases with different outcomes. Which difference
most challenges your current explanation?" After the reply, explain which premise
it strengthens or changes. If the missing piece is an external fact, investigate
it instead of asking the user to know it. Skip questions already answered.

Source: [Christine Padesky, Socratic Questioning: Changing Minds or Guiding Discovery?
(1993), pp. 4–6](https://padesky.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/socquest.pdf).
This is a methodological talk in cognitive therapy. Borrow the discovery and
synthesis practices without treating the user as a patient or claiming validated
effects for AI collaboration.

## Double Diamond: show where exploration is heading

Use when the inquiry keeps branching or a direction is ready for selection.
Distinguish exploring and framing the problem from exploring and assessing responses.
Make clear which understanding is stable enough for the present purpose and which
alternatives or evidence still matter. A small trial may reveal a better problem
statement, so revisiting earlier work is legitimate.

For example: "The intended outcome is clear enough to compare these two approaches;
the unresolved issue is how each behaves under the constraint you described."
Then compare them or seek evidence. Ask the user only for a material choice that
cannot be inferred. Do not require four completed stages or a product deliverable
from an open-ended inquiry.

Source: [Design Council, Framework for Innovation](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/framework-for-innovation/).
Its design process supplies an orientation for divergence and convergence, not a
test of factual truth or a prescribed number of conversational rounds.

## Value of information: choose what deserves more attention

For a decision, consider both how unresolved an issue is and whether learning more
could change a consequential judgment. Use this qualitative distinction to compare
research branches; high uncertainty by itself does not establish high priority.
Do not claim to calculate formal value of information without the required model.

State the connection yourself when the context supports it: "This unknown could
change which option fits your goal; resolving it is more useful now than refining
the detail both options share." If the connection is doubtful, take a small probe
when it could uncover a new frame or possibility, then reassess. Preserve interesting
leads with a reason to return. The user can choose exploration for its own sake.

Source: [Runge et al., A simplified method for value of information using constructed
scales (2023)](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/a-simplified-method-value-information-using-constructed-scales).
The published abstract separates decision relevance from uncertainty magnitude in
a formal method illustrated with conservation research priorities. Our conversational
heuristic borrows that distinction; it does not implement or validate that calculation.

## Intellectual standards: test the actual claim

Use during clarification, synthesis, or checking when a particular statement needs
scrutiny. Select the standard that exposes the current difficulty:

- **Clarity and precision:** identify the ambiguous term or missing detail and use
  a concrete example to establish what is being claimed.
- **Accuracy:** locate the observation or source that could verify a factual claim;
  use [research](research.md) when the fact is unknown.
- **Relevance and logic:** show how a reason bears on the question, or name the
  missing premise, contradiction, or inference that does not follow.
- **Depth and breadth:** examine a consequential missing factor or competing account,
  explaining how it could change the present understanding.
- **Fairness:** represent the strongest actual position and apply the same standards
  to the user, the coordinator, and other contributors.

Return a clearer claim, a supported correction, or a precise unresolved issue.
Do not recite every standard, score the user's intelligence, or require opposition.
Apply lighter exploratory challenge to a developing possibility and more exact
scrutiny to premises supporting a commitment or final conclusion.

Source: [Paul and Elder, Universal Intellectual Standards](https://www.criticalthinking.org/pages/universal-intellectual-standards/527),
especially the questions under the named standards. This is a normative guide to
examining reasoning; using its vocabulary does not establish accuracy or prove
that this skill improves reasoning.

## Test a consequential inference

Use when a claim would justify a commitment, a chosen means, or a final conclusion.
Identify the claim, its evidence, the premise connecting them, its conditions, and
what could defeat it. Explain the missing link when evidence does not reach the
conclusion; more sources cannot repair an invalid inference. Keep a source's
qualifiers and applicability limits through synthesis, summaries and handoffs.

For a proposed means, distinguish "could help", "fits these conditions" and
"is necessary". Try a result that achieves the same outcome without that means;
if plausible, necessity is unestablished, even if the means remains useful. Examine
the assistant's favored plan as carefully as the user's. Personal values need not
be proven as factual claims, and an explicitly required method remains a constraint
unless the user changes it.

For example, a user discussing collaboration failures describes a past project
where an early implementation idea became the whole conversation. Extract the
unsupported jump from desired outcome to required means. Do not design that
project unless the user actually changes the task. Equally, a clear request to
switch to that project should update the inquiry rather than be dismissed as drift.

Source: [Academic Research Skills, argumentation framework, inspected 2026-09-11](https://github.com/imbad0202/academic-research-skills/blob/c7af8b9017954c745ed7ad4afed4dba460a6c247/deep-research/references/argumentation_reasoning_framework.md).
This adapts its Toulmin components to inquiry and means selection. Its causal
criterion counts are not adopted; this adaptation has no established efficacy here.

## Revise a judgment for a reason

At a consequential challenge, state the current judgment and what would change it,
inspect the relevant evidence or counterexample, then report the resulting judgment
and its effect on the next action. Update the existing draft when material. This
applies to the assistant's choices as well as the user's claims; do not invent
prior predictions, beliefs or learning on the user's behalf.

A correction may repair the assistant's interpretation without changing the user's
goal. If the challenge fails, retain the supported judgment and explain why. If a
fact remains unknown, identify the observation needed; another round of agreement
does not resolve it. Reflection should produce an inspectable change or a supported
decision to retain the approach, not a promise to think harder. Do not require a
prediction exercise or forced disagreement in every turn.

Source: [Academic Research Skills, Socratic questioning and SCR, inspected 2026-09-11](https://github.com/imbad0202/academic-research-skills/blob/c7af8b9017954c745ed7ad4afed4dba460a6c247/deep-research/references/socratic_questioning_framework.md).
The source uses predictions and reflection for guided learning. Here the pattern
supports transparent judgment revision; it is not a user score or a validated
measure of learning or attention.

## Observe uptake and usefulness separately

At a meaningful checkpoint or resumption, use the user's actual explanation,
application, or reported experience to adjust the next contribution. Assimilated
personal Alpha can become Beta without resolving a factual dispute or changing
the idea's external distinctiveness. Do not infer assimilation from your summary
or a bare agreement, and do not impose a recall exercise after every turn.

When judging whether the interaction helped, distinguish the user's experience,
their demonstrated use of an idea, the quality of a particular judgment, and
transfer to other problems. Preserve positive feedback for what it establishes.
A changed opinion is not enough: inspect whether the change follows from better
evidence or reasoning. Retaining a well-supported view can also be progress.

Source: [An Experimental Comparison of Cognitive Forcing Functions for Execution
Plans in AI-Assisted Writing, sections 5.2, 5.4, and 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18033v1).
The experiment measures overreliance separately from perceived helpfulness and
reports that opinion revision alone is an insufficient effectiveness measure.
Its writing-task results do not validate this skill or establish that combining
more interventions improves outcomes. Select observations relevant to the current
purpose rather than adding a mandatory evaluation sequence to every conversation.

For disputed factual or causal claims, use [the research protocol](research.md) to
compare evidence and rival explanations. When distinct contributors can expose a
consequential blind spot, use [the roundtable protocol](roundtable.md). For a mature
candidate needing independent scrutiny, use [adversarial review](roundtable.md#review-a-mature-candidate)
directly. Method choice and institutional reputation do not replace inspecting evidence.
